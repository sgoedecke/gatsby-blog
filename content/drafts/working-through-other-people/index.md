working through other people is messy and often way more inefficient than just doing it

One thing I've been grappling with is how to balance the need to ship against concerns like technical leadership and fostering a team culture (especially as a senior-ish IC without any direct management authority) . I don’t think I’ve seen you talk about this yet.

For example, when conducting a PR review, I feel like I”m not just making a trade-off between, say, shipping and accruing some technical debt. I’m also thinking about what broader message I’m sending by what I let through and what I flag as an issue, and the impact this might have over time on team culture. I personally find those needs quite hard to balance.

Also, getting something from “not good enough” to “just about good enough” is a much messier process when doing it through another person. That person might not have the experience to see that distinction themselves, and communicating about code is naturally much more lossy than just writing it. Something that would be a half hour fix if done yourself can descend into several days worth of back and forth over code review. It's hard to know what to do here.

Overall I think there's a tradeoff between fostering a healthy team culture and delivering for the business. Those two goals are often in tension with each other. Where to sit on that spectrum? It's difficult. In ~2015, it was common to weight it 99-1 in favour of a healthy team culture. Today, the emphasis is back on delivering, and culture stuff is seen as optional. Everyone gets to decide where to draw the line, but if you're too out of sync with your company on that decision you're going to get punished for it.

There's an interesting distinction between working with other people and working through them. For me, it comes down to trust: do you trust someone to make sensible decisions autonomously (in which case you're working with them), or do you need to check up on each of their decisions (in which case you're working through them). You always have to do some working-through - for instance, if you're working with someone who's genuinely incompetent - but it's never pleasant. Far faster to just do the work yourself, but often you can't do that for organizational reasons: you'll make them feel bad, or the org needs to have them assigned to something, or there's a hope that they'll grow into someone you can eventually work with, and so on.
